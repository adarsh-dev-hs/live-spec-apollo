## How the work is delivered

| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| A working path before a thick one | Each category is taken end to end - one client, one requisition, one candidate sourced, ranked, shortlisted, placed, tracked to day 90 and invoiced - before any part of it is thickened. Nurses first, then doctors, then allied health. Module coherence remains a secondary aim because it is cheaper to build, but it gives way to getting a path working. |
| Gates every three to four weeks | Each gate is a working demonstration rather than a document. Named Apollo attendees: Shallu Kakkar for fulfilment and the recruiter workflow, Suman for the client-facing surfaces, Mr. Abednego Peter for clinical quality and the candidate record. A written record at each gate of what was raised and what changed. |
| Sign-off on what can be operated | Acceptance is tied to a gate producing something a stakeholder can use, not to a milestone being complete. |
| The scenario catalogue is authored jointly | The conditions each item must satisfy are settled gate by gate rather than fixed during design. Where feedback at a gate concerns capability already inside the agreed scope, it completes the specification for that capability. Where it introduces capability the plan does not describe, it is a change. |
| Three assumptions the gates exist to settle | Whether recruiters accept a ranked shortlist and what their overrides imply about the weighting; whether a hospital contact will click a link to confirm retention at day 90; and whether candidates install the app, since shift attendance has no other route. |

## Basis of the estimate

| Item | Basis |
|---|---|
| Working days | A month is taken as 21 working days. Six months is 126 working days per person. |
| Effort to calendar | Person-days divide by team size, then get checked against the build order and the couplings. Several milestones cannot run at the same time however many people are on them. |
| What is not in the figures | Discovery workshops, client sessions, defect cycles past first-pass QA, project management, and the 500 consulting hours in the legal SOW. UAT support is in M1 as a line item; the rest is not. |
| Confidence | Milestones M1 to M9 are sized from the client's Release 1 list. M10 to M15 are sized from capability names only, with no client chapter, and all their items carry Low confidence. |

> **On the team size figure.** Total effort divided by 126 working days gives the average team size needed to finish inside six months. That average is not a plan on its own: design front-loads into months one and two, QA back-loads into months five and six, and the integration-heavy items in M1, M4, M5 and M13 need specialists rather than more generalists. Treat the number as the floor for capacity planning, then shape the team around the build order.

> **The back half is compressed, and this is the main schedule risk.** Five of the ten build steps begin in months five and six: M8, then M9 and M11, then M10, M12 and M14, then M15, then M13. Each of those steps needs the one before it, so they cannot simply be run in parallel by adding people. Two consequences worth stating now. First, any slip in months one to four lands entirely on the Release 2 milestones, because they are the only things left to cut. Second, M13 payroll sits last behind three other milestones — if the contractual line matters commercially, it is the item most exposed to everything upstream of it.
