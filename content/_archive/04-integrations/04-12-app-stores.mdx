## 1. Purpose

Publishing the candidate app. **This is the only route by which shift attendance reaches the platform**,
so it sits directly on the contractual invoice chain — no app, no attendance, no timesheet, no shift
invoice.

The accounts must be **Apollo's**, which means the paperwork is Apollo's to complete, and **first
submission is reviewed more slowly than later updates.**

## 2. Scope

**In scope:** the EAS Build and Submit pipeline; store metadata, screenshots and privacy declarations;
release channels; OTA updates for JS-only changes; a placeholder build through review early to retire
the risk.

**Explicitly out of scope:** the app itself (`03-05`); push notification delivery (`02-09` via Expo).

**Explicitly not built:** enterprise or sideloaded distribution; a web fallback for check-in — the plan
is explicit that there is **no alternative attendance route**.

## 3. Plan traceability

No item of its own. Publishing is part of `M4-I1` and is named as a dependency of `M4-D3` and
`M8-D22`.

## 4. Dependencies

**Must exist first:** `03-05`, `02-12` (the privacy declarations both stores require).

**Consumed by:** `03-05`, and therefore `03-10` and the whole contractual chain.

**External:** `X12` Google Play Console and `X13` Apple App Store Connect — **organisation enrolment in
Apollo's legal name; Apple requires a D-U-N-S number from Dun & Bradstreet, which is not instant.**

## 6. State machines

### Release

`build (EAS) → submit → in review → approved | rejected → released`. A **rejection is calendar time no
team size removes**, which is why a placeholder build goes through review in Phase 2 rather than
discovering the review process in Phase 4.

**OTA** for JS-only changes bypasses review entirely; native changes do not.
