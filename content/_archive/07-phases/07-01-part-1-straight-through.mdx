## 1. Purpose

The first working version of the platform, carrying **one line of business straight through**. A
recruiter signs in; a hospital is set up with its rate card and contract terms; a vacancy is entered in
the console; a nurse signs up on her phone, uploads a CV and is checked against the nursing council;
she is ranked, shortlisted and put in front of the hospital on a link with no password; an offer goes
out; she joins; she is checked in on at day 0, 7, 30, 60 and 90; and the permanent invoice is raised
once the hospital confirms at day 90 that she is still there.

Everything in Part 1 sits on that sentence, plus the four things that
[cannot be retrofitted](/docs/07-phases/07-00-release-model#3-the-classification-rule).

### Explicitly out of scope

- **Contractual shift staffing** in every form: shift templates, demand lines, shift acceptance,
  check-in, attendance, timesheets and shift invoices. Part 2.
- **Doctors and allied health.** The category mechanism is built; only the nurse set is populated.
- **Home healthcare.** Care setting is reference data from day one; only `hospital` is populated.
- **Every intake channel but the console.** No email, WhatsApp, spreadsheet, HRMS or portal intake.
- **The client portal.** The only client surface is a tokenised link.
- **Every report.** The activity trail on a record is not a report.
- **Legacy migration**, engagement cadence, associate onboarding, workforce operations, payroll,
  collections, prioritisation and timesheet extensions.

### Explicitly not built

- A second weight set, a second registry, a second link type beyond the two named, or a second
  invoice type. Each is a Part 2 or Part 3 addition to the same tables.
- Automatic CV reading. A CV is uploaded and stored in Part 1 and **read by a recruiter**; the parser
  is Part 3.
- Automatic document reading. Documents are reviewed by eye; OCR is Part 3.

## 3. Scoped functionality

Ten functionalities. `F-P1-10` is listed last but **built first** — it is the foundation the other nine
stand on.

Screen references resolve against the POC base URL (`NEXT_PUBLIC_POC_APP_URL`, default
`https://live-app-apollo.vercel.app`) and **every reference the POC serves is a link — click it to open that
screen in the live app**. A screen the spec requires but the POC does not render is marked **not in
POC** and stays unlinked; that is a gap in the POC, never in scope.

| 4 | She does not answer by the escalation point | The event escalates, then is marked `missed`. It is never silently dropped |
| 5 | Day 90 arrives | A `day_90_retention` link is issued to the designated contact |
| 6 | The contact answers "yes, still here" | An immutable retention record with `basis` = `acknowledged`, their name, the time and the contract version |
| 7 | The contact does not answer within the window | `basis` = `deemed`, with the window and **whether it came from the client or the platform default** |
| 8 | The placement had no designated recipient | Flagged on the placements list **before** day 90, so it can be fixed |
| 9 | The nurse leaves at day 45 | Drop-out `left_early` with a reason; state `trial_ended`; **no billing trigger is created** |
| 10 | She never joins | `did_not_join`; the position returns to the requisition |
| 11 | Someone tries to answer day 30 or day 60 to trigger billing | **Refused.** Only the day-90 record can |
| 12 | The contract changes between joining and day 90 | The record carries the version **in force at the trigger**, and the invoice is priced on that |
| 13 | Someone edits an existing retention record | Refused. It is immutable |

#### Tested against

- **Happy** — a nurse joins, is checked in on at 0, 7, 30 and 60, the hospital confirms at day 90 on a
  phone, and an immutable retention record exists with their name against it.
- **All** — scenarios 1–13, plus: **day 30 and day 60 proven unable to reach the billing path**;
  deemed retention producing a valid invoice input; a drop-out at each of the five points; the trial
  events surviving a change to the check-in question set after scheduling.

## 4. Part 1 exit conditions

Part 1 is complete when all of the following are demonstrated on real data, not on fixtures.

| # | Condition | Proves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A named recruiter signs in, sees only their permitted navigation, and every action appears on the activity trail | `F-P1-1` |
| 2 | An administrator adds a candidate category **as data**, and it reaches three surfaces without a deployment | `F-P1-10`, and the whole Part 2 category argument |
| 3 | A contract is activated with its three windows and read back through the terms lookup | `F-P1-2` |
| 4 | A nurse is verified end to end through a real registry **or its manual fallback** | `F-P1-5` |
| 5 | A crude ranked list on three fields is put in front of recruiters, and **their overrides are captured as the weighting specification** | `F-P1-6`, and `OI-A-11` |
| 6 | **One client, one nurse, one link**: a tokenised day-90 link reaches a real hospital contact, is opened on a phone, answered, and produces an immutable retention record | `F-P1-7`, `F-P1-8` — **and the riskiest assumption in the engagement** |
| 7 | A permanent invoice fires from that record and is traceable to the confirmation in two clicks | `F-P1-9` |
| 8 | A release is promoted dev → prod without hand-edited config, and a restore is rehearsed | `F-P1-10` |

**Condition 6 is the one that matters most.** If a hospital contact will not click the link, the entire
permanent billing chain needs another trigger — and Part 1 is where that is found out, not Part 3.
