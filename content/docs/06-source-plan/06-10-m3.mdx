`Release 1` · `32 items` · `186 conditions` · `208.0pd` · `15 high risk` · `Ops/Admin`

Clients tell Apollo about vacancies in whatever way suits them — an email, a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp message, a WhatsApp voice note, a feed from their own HR system, or just by telling a recruiter who sits in their hospital. This module takes all of those and turns them into one standard requisition record. It checks whether the required fields are there, chases the client on the same channel they used if anything is missing, and then tracks the vacancy through its life: open, being worked on, paused, changed, partly filled, filled, closed. It also records who owns the vacancy and which recruiters were put on it during the daily morning call.

### Built once, used elsewhere

The message-receiving pipeline built here — receive, queue, work out who sent it, parse it, decide what kind of message it is, handle failures, retry — is built once in this module. M5 reuses the same pipeline for candidate replies. So it must be built to accept a second kind of message from the start, not just requisitions. It also carries two pieces of foundation work first needed here: the message-sending plumbing the follow-up chase runs on, and the event recording the M9 reports read later.

### Records this milestone owns

- InboundRecord — one row per incoming message or file. Keeps the channel, the sender, the time, and the original raw content. Kept forever, even after the requisition is created, so a disputed vacancy can be traced back to what the client actually sent.
- Requisition — the main record. Client, site, role, how many positions, candidate category, the category-specific fields, the rate to use, how complete it is, which fields are missing, who owns it, its status and its age.
- RequisitionVersion — written every time the requisition is changed, recording what changed, when, and who did it.
- ShiftDemandLine — contract staffing only. One row per shift, generated from a shift pattern.
- AllocationRecord — which recruiters were put on this vacancy, when, and by whom. Rows are only ever added, never changed.
- StatusTransition — every status change with a timestamp. M9 cannot report where candidates drop out without this.

### How records move

- InboundRecord: received → identified (we know the client) or unmatched (we do not). Then parsed, or unparseable. Then consumed once a requisition is made from it.
- Requisition: incomplete or complete → awaiting client (we are chasing missing fields) → complete → open → in fulfilment → partially filled → filled
- Requisition: in fulfilment ↔ on hold. Putting it on hold stops the age counter; releasing it restarts from where it stopped, not from zero.
- Requisition: any state → closed. A reason from the configured list is required.

### Not in this milestone

- Working out which vacancies matter most, and any approval step before a vacancy goes live. That is milestone M14.
- The system suggesting or balancing which recruiter gets which vacancy. Here we only record the decision made on the morning call.
- Enforcing a cap on how many vacancies a recruiter holds. The 15-16 norm is not enforced.
- The client portal as a way of sending vacancies in. Listed as a channel so it can be switched on later, but not built here.
- Automatically merging duplicate requisitions. We show a suspected duplicate and let a person decide.

### Needs to exist first

- M1 for logins and environment. M2 for the client, sites and rate card. M4 for the definition of the category-specific fields.

### What breaks without it

- M6 ranks against the requisition — no requisition, no ranking, and both business flows stop at step one. M7 offers and client links quote the requisition. M8 counts placements against it. M9 needs the owner and allocation to report by recruiter. M5 reuses the message pipeline built here.
