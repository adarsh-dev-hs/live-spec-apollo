questions: **what it is for**, **what it covers**, **what it deliberately does not cover**, and
**when it ships**.

designed once for its finished shape and switched on in slices — see the
[deliverable matrix](/docs/release-plan/scope/matrix).

### Clients, contracts and rate cards

**Delivers** `D1`, `D13` · **Released** Parts 1, 2, 3 · **Screens** `/clients`, `/clients/:id`,
`/contracts`, `/contracts/:id`

**Purpose.** The client side of the business: the hospital or home healthcare provider, its sites and
departments, the rates agreed with it, and the signed contract. Two things here matter more than they
look. The list of email addresses and WhatsApp numbers belonging to each client is what the inbound
pipeline checks every incoming message against to work out who sent a requisition. And the contract
terms are read **at the moment billing decides to raise an invoice** — with no fallback.

**Functionality.** Client entity with legal name, tax details, billing address, service lines and
**care setting** · locations and departments, home healthcare sites having no departments · client
contacts, including who receives which type of tokenised link · **sender identities** — email
addresses, WhatsApp numbers and client system tenants, each mapped to exactly one client · rate cards
by role, skill and location, versioned by effective date, carrying a **baseline rate** and the
placement fee percentage · contracts with versioning, renewal alerts and history · the three
per-client waiting periods, each with a platform default fallback · the two lookup services billing
depends on: rate resolution and contract terms in force.

**Not here.** Client login and self-service (client portal) · issuing links and rendering link pages
(client link surfaces — this module holds the contacts, that one holds the links) · invoicing.

**Not built at all.** Drafting contracts from a template — contracts are **recorded, not drafted**.
Credit limits and credit control. Electronic signature of the client contract: the signed document is
attached, and signing happens outside the platform.

### Demand intake

**Delivers** `A1` · **Released** Parts 2, 3 · **Screens** `/inbound`, `/inbound/unmatched`,
`/inbound/manual-entry`, `/duplicates`, `/admin/channels`

**Purpose.** Clients tell Apollo about vacancies in whatever way suits them — an email, a spreadsheet
in their own format, a WhatsApp message, a WhatsApp voice note, a feed from their own HR system, or
by telling a recruiter who sits in their hospital. This module turns all of them into one standard
requisition payload, scores it against what *complete* means for that candidate category, chases the
client **on the channel they used** for anything missing, and gives a person a queue for everything
the machine could not do.

**Functionality.** Four operational queues: inbound list, unmatched senders, manual entry, suspected
duplicates · channel configuration for six channels · per-client spreadsheet column mapping,
configurable without a deployment · required-field sets per candidate category, which define what
*complete* means · follow-up configuration — message per channel, window, escalation target · parser
dispatch and field extraction to the requisition schema · completeness scoring and the chase loop.

**Not here.** The shared receive-resolve-classify stack, which is
[inbound pipeline](/docs/engineering/build/platform-services#inbound-pipeline) — this module owns the
demand half: the parsers, the completeness rules, the chase and the human queues.

**Release shape.** Email, WhatsApp and the portal in Part 2; spreadsheets, client HRMS and voice
notes in Part 3.

### Requisitions and allocation

**Delivers** `D2`, `D14` (thin slice) · **Released** Parts 1, 2 · **Screens** `/requisitions`,
`/requisitions/new`, `/requisitions/:id`, `/board`

**Purpose.** The requisition: the single structured record every channel normalises to, and the
object every module downstream quotes. It tracks the vacancy through its life — open, in fulfilment,
paused, revised, partly filled, filled, closed — counts how long it has been open **excluding held
days**, and records who owns it and which recruiters were put on it at the daily morning call.

**Functionality.** The requisition form, including the verbal-capture entry point · the record view,
carrying its source evidence so a disputed requirement traces back · version history — **a revision
is a versioning event, not an edit** · the fulfilment board showing live demand, aging and fill in
one view · shift templates and the shift demand lines they generate · hold, revise and close, each
with distinct rules about the aging clock and mandatory reasons · owner and allocation, append-only ·
the requisition state machine and its append-only status-change log.

**Not here.** The allocation half is deliberately **a record, not an assignment engine**. The morning
call already makes the decision; this module writes it down. Prioritisation, recruiter suggestion and
approval are [demand prioritisation and approval](#demand-prioritisation-and-approval) in Part 3.

**Release shape.** Permanent requisitions in Part 1; shift templates, shift demand lines and the
`D14` owner and allocation capture in Part 2.

### Candidate record and credentials

**Delivers** `D4`, `D16`, `A2`, `A9` · **Released** Parts 1, 2, 3 · **Screens** `/candidates`,
`/candidates/:id`, `/verification`, `/verification/pendency`, `/learner-matches`

**Purpose.** Everything Apollo holds about a healthcare professional: who they are, which of the two
supply bases they belong to, the extra fields that differ by candidate category, their credentials
with issuer, validity period and verification method, and the verification team's work queue. **One
candidate record spans both supply bases against one shared credential object** — not a duplicated
record with a status flag.

**Functionality.** Candidate list and record, with availability confidence and last-confirmed time
visible · supply base membership — permanent pool, shift pool, or both — modelled explicitly ·
credentials with issuer, validity period and verification method, shared across both bases · the
verification queue with **pendency tracked by cause**, and the verification detail view · document
review against extracted text, with corrections retained so extraction accuracy can be measured ·
certification records and the learner-match queue · **the single definition of the per-category
attribute set**, served to the app, the console, demand and ranking so the two sides cannot drift
apart · duplicate detection and merge — the single merge implementation in the platform · check
orchestration through the market adapter layer, and credential expiry tracking.

**Not here.** The registry adapters themselves
([market adapter layer](/docs/engineering/build/platform-services#market-adapter-layer)) · consent, which
is verified before any check is dispatched
([data protection](/docs/engineering/build/platform-services#data-protection)).

**Release shape.** Scan-based Aadhar, PAN and certificate capture for one category in Part 1;
third-party verified checking and the remaining categories in Part 2; document extraction at width,
duplicates and expiry across the whole base in Part 3.

### Candidate app

**Delivers** `D3` · **Released** Parts 1, 2, 3 · **Screens** `/app/signup`, `/app/home`, `/app/jobs`,
`/app/shifts`, `/app/applications`, `/app/check-in`, `/app/availability`, `/app/documents`,
`/app/profile`, `/app/payslips`

**Purpose.** The React Native app healthcare professionals use: sign up with a phone number, build a
profile from a CV, find permanent jobs and shifts, apply and accept, track what is happening, upload
documents, answer availability questions, and **check in and out of a shift**.

**Functionality.** Sign-up by phone and OTP, category selection, consent · profile building from a CV
with review-and-correct, or by hand · category-specific attribute capture · job discovery and
application for Base A · shift discovery, acceptance and clash prevention for Base B · application
and shift tracking, interview confirmation, offer acceptance · **shift check-in and check-out with an
offline queue** · document upload for verification · notification settings and the per-user message
log · the in-app availability answer, **on the same schema as WhatsApp** · the app shell: navigation,
offline handling, push, release pipeline, help content · the configurable set of basic screening
questions asked at application, with answers collected against the candidate.

**Plan it against all three consumers.** `D3` is a shared build. Shift discovery, acceptance and
check-in serve attendance, and the app is one of the two channels candidate responses arrive on.
**Shift attendance is captured by app check-in with no alternative route: no app means no attendance,
no timesheet and no shift invoice.**

**Release shape.** Sign-up, profile, screening questions, jobs and documents in Part 1; shifts,
check-in and availability in Part 2; CV parsing at width and payslips in Part 3.

### Engagement and availability

**Delivers** `D29`, `A3`, `A4`, `A5`, `A14` · **Released** Parts 2, 3 · **Screens**
`/engagement/review`, `/engagement/calls`, `/engagement/runs`, `/segments`, `/admin/cadence`

**Purpose.** Apollo needs to know which professionals are actually available right now. This module
decides **who** to contact, **how often**, on **which channel** and with **which question set**,
delivers through outbound messaging, captures the answer on one structured schema whichever channel
it arrived on, writes it back to the candidate record, and **resets a freshness score that otherwise
decays**. If nobody hears from a candidate, the score falls and ranking places them lower.

**Functionality.** Segments and contact schedules — **six segments**, three categories against two
bases, each with its own cadence, channel, question set and staleness threshold · send windows and
frequency caps, with a projected volume and cost preview before a cadence is enabled · voice
campaigns: segment, question set, call window, retry policy · the review queue for free-text replies
the platform was not confident about, and the call log · availability confidence decaying on six
schedules and resetting on a confirmed response · cadence selection applying opt-outs and send windows
**at selection, not at send** · candidate response capture across WhatsApp, app and voice on one
schema · registration of the candidate-response intent set against the inbound pipeline · write-back
to the availability field, reconciling a response that arrived on two channels.

**Build the four together.** `A3`, `A4`, `A5` and `A14` form a closed loop and **none of the four is
independently operable**. Build any three and none does anything useful.

**Cost note.** Base B is contacted every 3–4 days across the whole base. This module is the largest
driver of metered provider spend in the platform, which is why the volume projection exists before a
cadence can be switched on.

### Matching and ranking

**Delivers** `D5`, `D18`, `A8` · **Released** Parts 1, 2, 3 · **Screens** `/search`, `/segments`,
`/requisitions/:id/ranking`, `/requisitions/:id/shortlist`, `/shortlists`,
`/admin/ranking-weights`

**Purpose.** Given a vacancy, produce a ranked list of candidates, best first, with a plain
explanation of why each ranked where they did and which requirements they do not meet. Weights differ
per candidate category and per supply base, so there are **at least six sets**. Recruiters can search
manually and can move someone up or down, and every override is recorded. When a client or interviewer
rejects someone with a structured reason, that reason feeds back and affects later rankings.

**Functionality.** Advanced candidate search on skill, location, experience and status, with saved
searches · talent segments, **shared with the engagement cadence model rather than duplicated** · the
ranked set: score, rationale, met / partly met / unmet criteria, staleness · recruiter override with
a mandatory reason, append-only · weight sets per category and base, versioned by effective date ·
the ranking engine, producing rationale **as part of scoring, not reconstructed afterwards** ·
feature extraction from credentials, category attributes, availability, certifications and outcomes ·
shift-level ranking against a shift demand line · outcome write-back from selection · shortlist
assembly and submission, which issues the client link.

**One design, not two.** The rejection taxonomy in [selection and offers](#selection-and-offers) and
the features this engine reads are agreed in a joint session **before either module is built**. If
the ranking model has no slot for outcome data, feeding rejections back later means rebuilding it.

**Release shape.** One weight set in Part 1; per-category and shift-level ranking in Part 2; tuning
and `A8` write-back in Part 3.

### Selection and offers

**Delivers** `D6`, `D20` · **Released** Parts 1, 2, 3 · **Screens** `/interviews`, `/offers`

**Purpose.** Interviews, the decision, and the offer. Propose slots to a candidate and a client
neither of whom has a login, handle reschedules and cancellations, capture structured feedback, then
accept, hold or reject with **a mandatory reason from a configured taxonomy** — which is what feeds
back into ranking. If accepted, generate the offer from a template selected by state, role and skill,
deliver it, track it to expiry, get it signed, and map it to the contract version whose terms billing
will read at day 90.

**Functionality.** Interview slot proposal to both sides, confirmation, reschedule, cancellation with
a reason · an interview list showing what is awaiting feedback, so the pipeline does not stall
silently · structured interview feedback that can feed the outcome write-back · accept / hold /
reject with a mandatory structured reason and a full selection audit trail · offer templates by
state, role and skill · offer generation, preview, delivery, tracking, expiry, reminders, and decline
with a reason · **offer-to-contract mapping, capturing the placement fee billing will read**.

**Why it is load-bearing.** Without offer management there is no offer acceptance and no joining
event, and joining, trial tracking, the retention link and retention-linked invoicing all stall
behind it.

**Release shape.** Generation, issue, tracking and acceptance in Part 1; templates by state, role and
skill plus reschedule handling in Part 2; digital signature in Part 3.

### Placement and trial

**Delivers** `D7`, `A6` · **Released** Parts 1, 2, 3 · **Screens** `/placements`, `/placements/:id`,
`/trial`

**Purpose.** Once someone accepts an offer, this module tracks them joining and then checks in on
them at **day 0, 7, 30, 60 and 90**. The candidate is asked whether they are being paid on time,
whether they have a preceptor and whether their accommodation is sorted. The client is asked at day
30 and 60 how it is going, and at day 90 **whether the person is still there** — that last answer is
what lets Apollo invoice.

**Functionality.** Joining confirmation, opening the placement and starting the 90-day clock · the
placement list with trial stage and billing-trigger visibility for finance · drop-out capture with a
reason, returning the position to the requisition · **privileging for doctors** — a hospital-granted
permission separate from council registration · the trial dashboard: which check-ins are done, due or
missed · check-in configuration: which events, which questions, **per candidate category and per care
setting** · candidate answers with escalation on a negative response · trial outcome and the visible
deemed-confirmation countdown · check-in scheduling on both sides, and question rendering across app,
WhatsApp and voice · **the immutable day-90 record**.

**The most consequential object in the platform.** The day-90 record must still make sense to someone
querying a charge a year later, so it is immutable, it snapshots the terms in force, and it records
whether the client acknowledged or the window simply expired.

**Care-setting difference.** Home healthcare has neither a preceptor nor accommodation, so its
question set differs — which is why the question sets are configuration and not a form.

### Attendance and timesheets

**Delivers** `D22` (thin slice) · **Released** Part 2 · **Screens** `/attendance`, `/timesheets`,
`/timesheets/:id`

**Purpose.** The contractual invoice chain. Shift attendance arrives from app check-ins — **there is
no other route** — exceptions are resolved by a person, and the period is compiled into a timesheet
priced against the contract version in force. The client approves it through a tokenised link, or it
is deemed approved when the window expires, and billing invoices against the **approved** timesheet
rather than raw attendance.

**Functionality.** Shift attendance — expected, checked in, checked out, no-show — with the location
stamp recorded · **geo-fencing set to allow, warn or block a check-in outside the boundary**, the
boundary being the site for facility work and the visit address for home visits · exception
resolution before a timesheet is compiled, including bulk resolution of a systemic cause · manual
time correction with a mandatory reason, audited because it changes an invoice · timesheet
compilation, pricing against the contract version, and reissue after correction · sending for
approval, which issues the timesheet link · timesheet tracking with the **visible deemed-approval
countdown** · the check-in receiver, including offline check-ins synced later · the approved-timesheet
export for the manual payroll process that runs until Part 3.

**Not here.** Leave categorisation, overtime treatment and internal approval routing —
[timesheet extensions](#timesheet-extensions), Part 3.

### Billing and receivables

**Delivers** `D8`, `A7`, `D25` (thin slice) · **Released** Parts 1, 2 · **Screens** `/invoices`,
`/invoices/:id`, `/billing/evidence`, `/receivables`

**Purpose.** Two invoice paths and the evidence behind each. A **permanent placement** invoices once
the client confirms at day 90 that the person is still there — not at placement. A **contractual
engagement**, including a locum doctor paid per session, invoices from a client-approved timesheet.
**The trigger follows the service line, not the candidate category.**

**Functionality.** Invoice list across both types, each linked to the evidence that allowed it ·
billing evidence: who confirmed, when, against which contract terms, and whether the client
acknowledged or the window expired · receivables, with ageing displayed · permanent invoice
generation from the day-90 record, at the terms in force at the trigger · the immutable day-90 record
store with snapshotted terms · contract-terms resolution at the trigger event, recorded on the
invoice · shift invoice generation from an approved or deemed-approved timesheet · duplicate
prevention on both paths.

**No fallback.** No contract terms in force at the trigger means permanent billing does not run at
all. That is not a defect to work around; it is the designed behaviour, and it is why contracts are
recorded before the first placement.

**Not here.** Payments, TDS, ageing chases and credit notes — [collections](#collections), Part 3. The
chain here ends at *invoice raised, receivable open*.

### Analytics and reporting

**Delivers** `D9`, `D28`, `A11` · **Released** Part 3 · **Screens** `/reports/funnel`,
`/reports/fill`, `/reports/trial-survival`, `/reports/stage-loss`, `/reports/performance`,
`/reports/rate-variance`, `/reports/monthly-pack`

**Purpose.** How many vacancies get filled, how fast, where candidates drop out, how each recruiter
and each intake channel performs, how many placements survive to day 90, and how the rate agreed
compares to the standard rate.

**Functionality.** Funnel and stage conversion · fill rate and time-to-fill, showing distribution
rather than only an average · trial survival across the full day 0–90 curve · stage-level conversion
loss by client and by recruiter · channel and recruiter performance · the monthly MIS pack, generated
on a schedule · rate agreed against baseline by client, role, specialty and acuity · **the mart layer
that makes all of the above possible**.

**Everything here reads marts built from the event spine, never operational tables.** And every one
of these reports depends on events having been written **when the work happened** — which is why the
event spine ships in Part 1 with nothing yet reading it.

**Dependency.** Stage-level loss by recruiter needs the `D14` owner field, captured from Part 2.

### Legacy migration

**Delivers** `A10` · **Released** Part 3

**Purpose.** Move Apollo's existing candidate data out of CPAL and the current tooling into the
platform, after which **the platform becomes the system of record and the old tool is switched off**.

**Functionality.** Data profiling: volume, completeness, duplicate rate, and a mapping feasibility
assessment · the migration pipeline — extract, map, load, resumable and re-runnable without creating
duplicates · delta runs picking up source changes between the main load and cutover · duplicate
resolution within the migrated base **and between the migrated base and app registrations** · the
operational screens: batch status, failed records, correction and rejection · reconciliation against
the source total, which is what permits cutover.

**The item with the least known about it.** Nobody has seen the data: how many records, how complete,
how many duplicates, whether fields map cleanly. **Its effort basis awaits joint data profiling**, so
profiling is the first task and belongs much earlier than the rest of the module.

### Client portal

**Delivers** `D12` · **Released** Part 2 · **Screens** `/portal`, `/portal/requisitions`,
`/portal/requisitions/new`, `/portal/shortlists`, `/portal/timesheets`, `/portal/invoices`

**Purpose.** A logged-in website for clients. Until Part 2 clients have only had one-off links with
no password; this gives them accounts so they can raise vacancies themselves, see how their vacancies
are progressing, review shortlists, approve timesheets and download invoices. Once it exists the
portal also becomes the **sixth intake channel**.

**Functionality.** Raise a requisition, producing **the same record as one arriving by email**, marked
as sourced from the portal · my requisitions, with client-appropriate status and fill · shortlists:
the logged-in version of the shortlist link, same decisions and same taxonomy · timesheets: the
logged-in version of the approval link, same state and same window rule · invoices: download their
own, and view rate card and contract read-only · enabling the portal channel in demand intake as
configuration.

**This module adds screens and a tenancy boundary, not new business logic.** Everything it shows
already exists. The one thing that is genuinely new is **hard row scoping** — a portal user reaches
their own client and sites and nothing else, and a new endpoint fails closed.

### Associate onboarding

**Delivers** `D21` · **Released** Parts 2, 3 · **Screens** `/workforce/onboarding`,
`/app/onboarding`

**Purpose.** The paperwork side of someone joining as a shift worker. Today it is collected and
stored outside the platform; this module brings it in.

**Functionality.** Associate profile: employment details, bank details, statutory identifiers,
completeness · statutory forms including PF/UAN transfer and gratuity nomination, each with its own
completion state and returnable with a reason · a document checklist differing by role and by state ·
the guided in-app onboarding flow · **the onboarding gate that blocks a pay run for an associate whose
paperwork is incomplete**.

That gate is the operational reason the module exists.

### Workforce operations

**Delivers** `D23` · **Released** Part 3 · **Screens** `/workforce/requests`

**Purpose.** The day-to-day changes that happen once people are working: salary revisions for one
person or in bulk, salary advances, and putting a salary on hold or releasing it. Each needs two
people — one to request and one to approve — **so nobody can change their own pay**.

**Functionality.** The maker-checker request engine, with request types as configuration, thresholds
by value, and raiser never equal to approver · salary revision, individual and bulk · salary advances
with a recovery plan, and holds with release · attendance upload from outside, paired against app
check-ins with mismatch resolution · geo-fence configuration and enforcement, for both a fixed site
and a per-visit address.

**Build it before timesheet extensions.** The request-and-approve engine here is reused for internal
timesheet approval; built the other way round, it gets built twice.

### Payroll

**Delivers** `D24` · **Released** Part 3 · **Screens** `/workforce/payroll`, `/app/payslips`

**Purpose.** Paying the shift workers. This connects the platform to a **third-party payroll engine —
the platform does not build a payroll calculator**. It assembles the inputs the engine needs,
configures the pay cycle, takes the results back, produces the PF and ESIC reports and challans, and
delivers payslips in the app and over WhatsApp.

**Functionality.** The pay run: open a cycle, lock inputs, send, receive results, issue payslips,
close · payroll settings: cycle frequency, cut-off dates, field mapping to the engine, deduction
rates · statutory reports: PF and ESIC reports and challans for a period · payslips in the app and
over WhatsApp · the engine integration boundary: assemble inputs, take back outputs, report
exceptions.

**Start it in Quarter 1 even though it ships in Quarter 3.** Engine selection, contract and field
mapping are client dependencies, and the module sits behind associate onboarding, attendance and
workforce operations. Until it exists payroll is done by hand, which puts a hard ceiling on how many
shift workers Apollo can take on.

**Not built.** Statutory filing. Reports and challans are produced; filing is outside the scope.

### Demand prioritisation and approval

**Delivers** `D14` (remainder) · **Released** Part 3

**Purpose.** [Requisitions and allocation](#requisitions-and-allocation) records which recruiter was
put on which vacancy, as decided on the morning call. This module makes the system do the deciding:
score which vacancies matter most, suggest which recruiter should take each one, and add an approval
step before a vacancy goes live.

**Functionality.** Priority scoring per requisition on urgency, value and age, with the reasoning
shown · the priority board, and manual override of a priority with a reason · suggested recruiter,
with current load shown against the operating norm · the requisition approval workflow — a new state
between created and open · the scoring and load-balancing engine.

**The system suggests; a person still decides.** Nothing here writes an allocation directly.

**Why Part 3 rather than next to the requisition.** Priority scoring and recruiter suggestion read
performance data that [analytics and reporting](#analytics-and-reporting) produces. Building this
earlier means building a suggestion engine against data that does not exist yet.

### Timesheet extensions

**Delivers** `D22` (remainder) · **Released** Part 3

**Purpose.** The three things the thin-slice timesheet deliberately left out.

**Functionality.** Leave types and leave records · overtime rules, varying by client and by state ·
internal timesheet approval routing, **reusing the workforce-operations approval engine** · the full
timesheet calculation applying leave and overtime **on top of** the Part 2 attendance data.

The same timesheet then feeds both the client invoice and the payroll input, **with no second
calculation**.

### Collections

**Delivers** `D25` (remainder) · **Released** Part 3 · **Screens** `/receivables`

**Purpose.** The money coming back in. Billing ends at *invoice raised, receivable open*; this module
records whether Apollo actually got paid.

**Functionality.** Payments received, matched across several invoices, with part-payments and
unmatched payments queued · TDS recorded separately so gross and net are both visible · an overdue
report with ageing **calculated from the contract payment terms**, not a flat rule · chase history, so
a client is not chased twice · automatic dunning on a schedule, stopping the moment payment arrives ·
credit and debit notes, approved before issue and exported alongside invoices.
