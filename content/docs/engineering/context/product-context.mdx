## 1. Two lines of business, behaving differently

Apollo Knowledge Services places healthcare professionals into hospitals and home healthcare
providers. It runs two lines of business at once, and they behave completely differently.

| | Permanent placement | Contractual (shift) staffing |
|---|---|---|
| What is sold | A person, hired by the client | Hours worked, invoiced by the shift |
| Supply base | **Base A** — the permanent pool | **Base B** — the shift pool |
| Availability means | A notice period | Time windows within a travel radius |
| How Apollo is paid | A percentage of CTC, invoiced **at day 90** once the client confirms the person is still there | Shift-level invoice from a **client-approved timesheet** |
| Billing trigger | The immutable retention record, written when a client answers a link | A timesheet in state `approved` or `deemed_approved` |
| Attendance | Not tracked — the person is the client's employee | App check-in, the **only** route |
| Payroll | Not Apollo's | Apollo's, run through a third-party engine from Part 3 |
| Released in | Part 1 | Part 2 |

**A locum doctor is a contractual engagement even though the candidate category is doctor.** The
billing trigger follows the service line, not the candidate category. Getting this wrong invoices
the wrong way for a whole class of placement.

## 2. The two value flows

### Permanent — Part 1

```
client onboarded → contract signed, with terms and waiting periods
  → requisition created → owner set, recruiters allocated on the morning call
  → ranked shortlist produced → sent to the client as a tokenised link
  → client answers yes, no or with comments → interview held, structured feedback captured
  → accept / hold / reject with a mandatory structured reason
  → offer generated from a template, delivered, signed, mapped to a contract version
  → joining confirmed → placement opens, the 90-day clock starts
  → check-ins at day 0, 7, 30, 60 and 90, candidate side and client side
  → day 90: the client confirms, or the deemed-confirmation window expires
  → immutable retention record  →  permanent invoice raised, receivable opened
```

### Contractual — Part 2

```
client onboarded → contract with shift rates and a deemed-approval window
  → shift requisition and shift template → one demand line per shift
  → candidates ranked against the shift line, Base B weights, travel radius, no clash
  → candidate accepts the shift in the app
  → candidate checks in and out in the app, location stamped, geo-fence applied
  → exceptions resolved → timesheet compiled and priced against the contract version
  → timesheet sent to the client as a link → approved, or deemed approved on expiry
  → shift invoice raised, receivable opened
```

Both chains stop dead without the tokenised link. The contractual chain stops dead without the
mobile app.

## 3. Who uses it

### Apollo internal — the only people with a login before Part 2

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| Recruiter | Work requisitions, search and shortlist, run interviews, capture verbal demand at the hospital |
| Fulfilment lead | Runs the daily morning call, allocates recruiters, watches the fulfilment board and trial dashboard |
| Business development | Clients, contracts, rate cards, client contacts and link recipients |
| Verification and quality | Work the verification queue, review document extraction, resolve credential checks |
| Finance | Invoices, billing evidence, receivables, timesheet tracking |
| Platform administrator | Settings, reference lists, channels, templates, weights, adapters |

Around twenty-two recruiters, most of them resident inside client hospitals. **Verbal capture of a
vacancy is a normal path, not an edge case.**

## 5. Category and care setting are dimensions, not releases

The SOW is explicit: **category is a dimension across the deliverables rather than a separate
deliverable.** Three categories against two supply bases gives **six supply segments**, each with
its own acquisition motion, decay schedule, contact cadence and ranking weight set.

| Category | Attribute set (`A9`) |
|---|---|
| Nurse | Specialty, acuity |
| Doctor | Specialty, case-mix, grade |
| Allied health | Discipline, equipment, technique competency |

| Care setting | Consequences |
|---|---|
| Hospital | Has departments, a preceptor, a fixed site; the geo-fence reference is the site |
| Home healthcare | **No department, no preceptor, no accommodation.** The workplace is a patient residence that changes between shifts, so the visit address sits on the shift line and is the geo-fence reference |

Both are held as editable reference data in [settings](/docs/engineering/build/platform-services#settings-and-reference-data),
and **nothing anywhere hardcodes a category**.

## 6. Operating facts that change the design

| Fact | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| The daily morning call already allocates recruiters — the platform **records the decision, it does not make it** until Part 3 | [Requisitions and allocation](/docs/engineering/build/modules#requisitions-and-allocation) |
| Base B is contacted **every 3–4 days across the whole base** | [Engagement and availability](/docs/engineering/build/modules#engagement-and-availability) — and it is the main metered-provider cost driver |
| Most recruiters sit inside hospitals, so verbal capture is routine | [Demand intake](/docs/engineering/build/modules#demand-intake) |
| WhatsApp template approval takes weeks and is outside Apollo's control | [Integrations](/docs/engineering/reference/integrations) — gates cadence go-live |
| App store review adds calendar time no team size removes | [Integrations](/docs/engineering/reference/integrations) — gates all shift attendance |
| Payroll runs by hand until Part 3, which caps contractual volume | [Attendance and timesheets](/docs/engineering/build/modules#attendance-and-timesheets) carries a timesheet export for it |

## 7. What the platform is not

- **Not the accounting system of record.** The chain ends at *invoice raised, receivable open* until
  Part 3 adds collections.
- **Not a payroll calculator.** A third-party engine calculates; the platform sends inputs and stores
  outputs.
- **Not a filing agent.** PF and ESIC reports and challans are produced; filing is outside the scope.
- **Not an LMS.** Certification records are held and can be received; the LMS is Apollo's own build.
- **Not a client-facing analytics product.** Clients see links, and from Part 2 a portal.
