Twenty-two things the platform needs from outside the delivery team. These are **separate from the open
decisions** in `05-03`: those are decisions about what to build, these are accounts, contracts,
registrations and access that must exist before a build can start or a supplier can be called.

**Almost all of them need someone at Apollo, not the delivery team.**

## 1. Start these in week one

Five, regardless of when their phase begins. The first four are **pure waiting on someone else's
timetable and no amount of delivery capacity shortens them**. The fifth changes what gets built, so it
is cheapest to answer before anything is built at all.

| Ref | What | Why week one |
|---|---|---|
| `X01` | Meta business verification for WhatsApp | Provider review plus per-template approval, weeks, outside Apollo's control |
| `X02` | DLT registration for SMS | Indian telecom regulator lead time |
| `X14` | Verification aggregator contract | Weeks of contracting and customer onboarding before a single call can be made |
| `X12`/`X13` | The two app store accounts | Apple needs a D-U-N-S number in Apollo's legal name, obtained from Dun & Bradstreet |
| `X06` | Whether the console federates to Apollo's identity provider | **Changes what `02-01` builds.** Retrofitting after ninety users are live is worse than either option |

### Via Apollo's clients — the slowest track

Two hops rather than one: Apollo asks their client, the client agrees, and only then can the work be
sized.

| Ref | What | Needed by | Who acts | Required from Apollo | Blocks | Open item |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `X27` | Which client HR systems, and how many | Month 2 | Apollo BD, then their hospital clients | The list, and each client IT team's agreement | `04-07` | `OI-M3-03` |
| `X28` | Client spreadsheet format samples | Month 2 | Apollo fulfilment, then their hospital clients | **Real files, not idealised ones** — the value of A1 is that it takes what clients already send | `04-08` | `OI-M3-04` |
| `X29` | Client link recipients | Month 4 | Apollo BD, then their hospital clients | Decide recipient scope; collect the named person and contact details at each client; **confirm each client is content to answer the day-90 question by link** | `02-14`, `03-11` | `OI-A-12` |

**`X29` is the one to watch.** The day-90 answer must be timestamped against an identified person for
the invoice to be defensible in a billing query. **Without a recipient model there is no defensible
trigger**, and the whole permanent billing chain rests on it.

## 5. How this is tracked

Each dependency has a named actor at Apollo, a month it is first needed, and the components it blocks.
It is reviewed **at every gate** (`00-05` §2), not at a monthly meeting — a dependency that slips is a
schedule fact, and the gate is where schedule facts are surfaced with the people who can act on them.

A dependency that has not moved by the month before it is needed is escalated as a **delivery risk with
a named consequence**, in the terms of `05-01` §9: what stops, and what gets cut if it stays stuck.
