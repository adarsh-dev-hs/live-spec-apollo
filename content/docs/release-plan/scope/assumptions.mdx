## 1. Assumptions

- **The platform is operated by the Client's internal teams.** External client-portal users for
  hospitals and home healthcare providers are onboarded when `D12` is delivered — in **Part 2**.
- **Candidate categories in scope are nurses, doctors and allied health professionals.** Category is
  a dimension across the deliverables rather than a separate deliverable; the speciality taxonomy is
  absorbed into `A9`.
- **The payroll engine is a third-party product integrated by the Service Provider.** Its licence,
  statutory updates and configuration for additional states are the Client's responsibility.
- **The functional boundary** is the scope set out in SOW §1.1 and the corresponding conditions in
  the agreed scenario catalogue; anything else is a Change.
- **Detailed user interface design, workflow rules and rate-card structures** are confirmed during
  design and shared with the Client.

### What each assumption means for the build

| Assumption | Engineering consequence |
|---|---|
| Apollo's own teams operate the platform | Only Apollo staff have a login before Part 2. Every client interaction in Part 1 is a tokenised link with no account — see [Client link surfaces](/docs/engineering/build/platform-services#client-link-surfaces). |
| Category is a dimension, not a deliverable | Categories and care settings are **editable reference data from day one**, never code. A core built to nurses alone makes doctors a migration rather than a configuration change. |
| Payroll is third-party | The platform assembles inputs and stores outputs. **It does not calculate pay.** |
| Design is confirmed during design | Screen-level detail is agreed inside the part it ships in, against the design system rather than per screen. |

## 2. Client dependencies

Four groups. Each is something only Apollo can provide, and each has a lead time that is not
recoverable by adding people.

### Third-party services and credentials

Timely provision of, and payment for:

| Service | Needed for | Needed by |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and credential checking — Aadhaar via DigiLocker, PAN, EPFO, court records | `D4`, `D16`, `A12` | Part 1 |
| Document reading | `D4`, `D3` | Part 1 |
| Messaging — WhatsApp Business Platform, SMS with **DLT registration**, email | `F07`, `D10`, `A1`, `A13` | Part 1 for sending; Part 2 for intake and cadence |
| Outbound voice and transcription | `A5`, `A1` | Part 2 |
| Electronic signature | `D20` | Part 3 |
| Background verification aggregator | `D16` third-party verified checking | Part 2 |
| Third-party payroll engine | `D24` | Selection needed in Part 1; live in Part 3 |

**WhatsApp template approval and DLT registration are regulator and platform lead times outside
anyone's control.** They are started early or they gate the part that needs them.

### Environment and accounts

- The **AWS Mumbai environment**, access and security tooling.
- **Apple and Google developer accounts** required to publish the candidate application.

The developer accounts must be in Apollo's name, and first submission is reviewed more slowly than
subsequent ones. **The candidate app is the only route by which shift attendance reaches the
platform**, so a store delay is a Part 2 delay, and no amount of engineering removes it.

### Decisions, content and people

- Design decisions.
- Notification template content.
- Acceptance testing resources, and approvals within the Acceptance Period.

Named recruiters carry 15–16 vacancies each. **UAT needs protected time booked in advance**, not
goodwill on the day.

### Legacy access

- Access to the **legacy candidate system** for profiling and migration.
- **A named person who can answer questions on its data.**

`A10` cannot be sized at all until profiling has run. It is a Part 3 deliverable whose first task
belongs much earlier than Part 3.

## 3. When a dependency is late

> Where a Client dependency is delayed and directly causes the Service Provider to be delayed or
> incur additional cost, the timeline and Fees shall be equitably adjusted through a Change under the
> master services agreement, provided the Service Provider promptly notifies the Client and uses
> reasonable efforts to mitigate.

Two obligations sit on the Service Provider in that sentence, and both are operational, not legal:
**notify promptly**, and **mitigate**. In practice that means every dependency above is tracked with
a needed-by date against the part it gates, and a delay is raised when it is observed rather than
when it bites.

## 4. The dependencies most likely to hurt

| Dependency | Why it is the one to watch |
|---|---|
| WhatsApp Business Platform registration and template approval | Gates intake, cadence, offer delivery, trial check-ins and payslips. Weeks of external lead time, no workaround. |
| Apple and Google developer accounts | Gate the candidate app, which gates all shift attendance, all timesheets and all shift invoices. |
| Verification aggregator selection | Several councils have no API and resolve to document plus manual verification; without the aggregator the rest is manual too. |
| Payroll engine selection | Field mapping cannot start until the engine is chosen, and payroll sits behind three other deliverables. |
| Legacy system access and a named data owner | Until profiling runs, `A10` has no credible effort basis. |
| Contract terms recorded before the first day-90 trigger | **Permanent billing has no fallback.** No terms in force means no invoice. |
