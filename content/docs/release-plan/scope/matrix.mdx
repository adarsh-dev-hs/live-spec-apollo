in different parts.

| `D25` | Shift invoice from a client-approved timesheet | Payment receipt, reconciliation with part-payments and TDS, ageing, dunning, credit and debit notes |

## 3. Reading the matrix as a build instruction

**A row with three dots is not three builds.** It is one component built once, released in slices.
The component is designed for its final shape from the start — the Part 1 slice of `D16` still has
the credential object with issuer, validity period and verification method, because retrofitting
that in Part 2 costs more than building it once.

What the slice controls is **what is switched on and put in front of a user**, not what the data
model can express. Two consequences follow, and they are the difference between the plan holding and
not:

1. **Build the final data shape, release the current slice.** `A2` models supply-base membership as
   membership in Part 1 even though only Base A is populated, because the SOW says explicitly it is
   not a status flag.
2. **Do not build past the slice.** Finishing `D22` in Part 2 because the module is open costs Part
   2's schedule for capability nobody asked for until Part 3.
