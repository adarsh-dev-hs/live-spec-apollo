**Due: end of Quarter 1.** Permanent placement live end to end for one client.

Part 1 is one complete line of business, narrow and finished, rather than a wide layer of half-built
capability. One client, one candidate category, one care setting, one way in and one way out.

## 1. What becomes available

As the SOW states it.

| Capability | What Part 1 carries |
|---|---|
| **Demand management** | The requisition object and its lifecycle — creation, versioning, hold, revise, close — and the fulfilment board with aging. |
| **Sourcing** | The candidate app asks the relevant basic screening questions, configured, and collects the answers. |
| **Verification** | Aadhar, PAN and certificates captured by scan. Configurable identity management and verification attributes, based on candidate category (type of candidate), destination (from the demand) and regulatory requirements (placement location and supply location). |
| **Matching** | Surfacing from the internal database, ranked. |
| **Offer** | Offer generation and issue. |
| **Onboarding** | Onboarding of the placed candidate. |
| **Day-90 retention invoice** | Raised on the day-90 retention record, configurable. |
| **Client engagement** | Client feedback as yes, no or with comments; verification of details; configured reminders carrying a warning of auto-accept after 7 days; configurability client by client. |

## 2. The chain, in order

```
client and contract recorded, with rate card and waiting periods
  → requisition created in the console, owner set
  → candidate signs up in the app, answers the screening questions, uploads documents
  → Aadhar, PAN and certificates read from a scan; credential checks run per category
  → ranked shortlist produced from the internal database
  → shortlist sent to the client as a tokenised link  →  yes / no / with comments
  → interview held, structured decision recorded with a mandatory reason
  → offer generated from a template, issued, accepted
  → joining confirmed; placement opens and the 90-day clock starts
  → day 90: the client confirms on a link, or the configured window expires
  → immutable retention record written  →  permanent placement invoice raised
```

Both ends of that chain are worth stating plainly:

- **There is no client login in Part 1.** Every client interaction is a tokenised link sent on a
  channel the client already uses, opened without a password or an account.
- **There is no fallback on permanent billing.** No contract terms in force at the trigger moment
  means the invoice does not run at all.

## 5. Deliberately absent from Part 1

Naming these matters as much as naming what is in — each is a thing a reader will look for.

| Absent | Arrives in |
|---|---|
| Demand arriving by email, WhatsApp, spreadsheet or client HRMS — every requisition is keyed in | Part 2 (`A1`) |
| Shift staffing: shift lines, shift acceptance, check-in, timesheets, shift invoices | Part 2 (`D22`, `D25`) |
| Doctors and allied health; home healthcare as a care setting | Part 2 (`A9`, `A2`) |
| A client login of any kind | Part 2 (`D12`) |
| Notification templates, triggers and preferences — Part 1 has the sending infrastructure (`F07`) but not the D10 layer on top | Part 2 (`D10`) |
| Automated candidate contact: cadence, decay, voice, response capture | Part 2 (`A3`, `A4`, `A5`, `A14`) |
| Third-party verified credential checking | Part 2 (`D16`) |
| Reporting of any kind. Events are recorded from day one; nothing reads them yet | Part 3 (`D9`, `D28`) |
| Payroll, workforce operations, collections, legacy migration | Part 3 |

**`F14` is the exception that proves the rule.** Nothing in Part 1 reads an event, and the event
spine is still built and live from the first requisition — because a report in Part 3 cannot cover a
period for which no history was written.

## 6. Part 1 is done when

1. A recruiter signs in, sees only their permitted navigation, and every action they take appears on
   an audit trail.
2. A vacancy for one client reaches a ranked shortlist without leaving the console.
3. A candidate registers in the app, answers the configured screening questions, and has Aadhar, PAN
   and a certificate read from a scan.
4. A client opens a tokenised link with no account and answers yes, no or with comments.
5. An offer is generated, issued and accepted, and a placement opens with the 90-day clock running.
6. At day 90 the client confirms — or the configured window expires with the warning already sent —
   and an immutable retention record produces a permanent placement invoice against the contract
   terms in force.
7. The whole of that runs in Apollo's own AWS Mumbai account, with a rehearsed restore evidenced
   before go-live.
