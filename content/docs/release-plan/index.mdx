## 3. How to read a part

Each part page carries three things:

1. **What becomes available** — in the client's own language, as the SOW states it.
2. **The deliverables that part draws on** — with `(partial)` where the deliverable is split
   across more than one part.
3. **What is deliberately absent** — the capability a reader might expect and will not find.

A deliverable marked `(partial)` is delivered across more than one part and appears in each part
in which work on it falls. Twenty-one of the fifty-one are split this way; they are listed in the
[deliverable matrix](/docs/release-plan/scope/matrix).

**Building a deliverable to completion when its part only asked for a slice is the most expensive
mistake available on this engagement.** Read the part before deciding how much of a module to build.

## 4. The rule that decides what goes in Part 1

Part 1 takes **one client, one candidate category, one line of business, one care setting** from
sign-in to a raised invoice. Anything on that line is in. Anything that widens it — a second
channel, a second category, home healthcare, a client login — waits for Part 2. Anything that
deepens it — reporting, payroll, collections, migration — waits for Part 3.

That is why the twelve `F` prerequisites are all in Part 1 and nowhere else: they are the floor the
first line stands on, and there is no thinner version of them worth building.
