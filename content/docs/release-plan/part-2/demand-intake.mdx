## Purpose

> Introduction to the feature. **This is not the scope** — the scope is items 1 to 7 below.

Clients tell Apollo about vacancies in whatever way suits them — an email, a WhatsApp message, or a
form on their own portal. This feature turns all of them into **one standard requisition payload**,
scores it against what *complete* means for that candidate category, chases the client **on the
channel they used** for anything missing, and gives a person a queue for everything the machine could
not do. Until now every requisition was keyed in by a recruiter; from here the recruiter's job on
most vacancies is to check what arrived rather than to type it.

## Release in this phase

1. The shared inbound pipeline: receive, record, deduplicate, resolve sender, classify, dispatch.
2. Email intake.
3. WhatsApp intake.
4. Portal intake as a registered channel.
5. Parser dispatch and field extraction to the requisition schema.
6. Completeness scoring and the chase loop.
7. The four operational queues.

<Callout type="warn" title="The pipeline must accept a second consumer from the start">
`A1` owns this stack and `A14` — candidate response capture in
[engagement and availability](/docs/release-plan/part-2/engagement-and-availability) — consumes it
with a different intent schema. **Build the intent-registration contract in item 1, not as a
retrofit** once demand parsing works.
</Callout>

## 1. The shared inbound pipeline

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/inbound`

### Scenarios to cover

Everything that arrives from outside lands here first: it is written down exactly as it was sent, a
redelivery does not become a second record, the sender is resolved to exactly one client, the message
is classified, and it is handed to whoever registered for that kind of message — retrying, and
**never discarding anything on the way**.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inbound record | Store | Yes | One per message or file, with the **raw payload retained forever** |
| 2 | Channel receiver | Component | Yes | One per channel, writing the same record shape |
| 3 | Deduplication | Behaviour | Yes | A provider redelivery updates, never duplicates |
| 4 | Sender identity resolution | Mechanism | Yes | Against the client sender list — **exactly one client, or the unmatched queue** |
| 5 | Intent classification | Mechanism | Yes | With a shared confidence threshold and a fallback |
| 6 | Intent registration contract | API | Yes | Lets a second consumer attach without modifying the stack |
| 7 | Reclassification | Action | Yes | Between intent sets, **without re-ingestion** |
| 8 | Queue, retry, dead letter | Job runtime | Yes | Observable end to end |

### Flow

1. A receiver accepts a message and writes the inbound record with its raw payload.
2. Deduplication checks the provider's message identifier.
3. Sender resolution matches against sender identities; no match goes to the unmatched queue.
4. Classification assigns an intent; below the confidence threshold it goes to the fallback.
5. Dispatch hands the record to the consumer registered for that intent.
6. Failures retry and then dead-letter, visible on `/admin/jobs`.

### Scenarios to test

- The raw payload is retained and readable for every record, including failures.
- A redelivered provider message produces one record, not two.
- A sender matching two clients is refused as ambiguous rather than assigned to one.
- A second intent consumer registers without a change to the pipeline.
- Reclassifying a record does not re-ingest it or lose its history.
- Nothing is discarded: every record ends in a terminal state that a person can see.

## 2. Email intake

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/inbound` · `/admin/channels`

### Scenarios to cover

Requisitions arrive as email into a monitored mailbox. The sender address resolves to a client, the
body and attachments are parsed, and a requisition is produced. A mail from an address nobody has
registered goes to the unmatched queue with a person told about it.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mailbox configuration | Channel config | Yes | Address, credentials, polling or push |
| 2 | Message capture | Receiver | Yes | Headers, body, attachments, all retained |
| 3 | Attachment handling | Behaviour | Yes | Stored through the shared file store |
| 4 | Thread handling | Behaviour | Yes | A reply attaches to the same follow-up thread |
| 5 | Sender resolution | Shared mechanism | Yes | From the address, against sender identities |
| 6 | Auto-reply suppression | Behaviour | Yes | Out-of-office and bounce messages do not create demand |

### Flow

1. Mail arrives in the monitored mailbox and is captured with its attachments.
2. The sender address resolves to a client; unmatched goes to the unmatched queue.
3. The classifier decides it is demand, and the parser in item 5 runs.
4. A reply to a chase message is threaded to the follow-up it answers.

### Scenarios to test

- A mail with attachments produces one record with every attachment stored.
- An out-of-office reply does not create a requisition.
- A reply threads to its follow-up rather than creating a new demand.
- An unknown sender lands in unmatched and a person is notified.
- Duplicate delivery of the same message produces one record.

## 3. WhatsApp intake

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/inbound` · `/admin/channels`

### Scenarios to cover

A client contact sends a WhatsApp message about a vacancy. The number resolves to a client, the
message is parsed, and a requisition is produced. **The same WhatsApp registration and template set
serves demand follow-up and candidate messaging** — one registration, not two.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | WhatsApp channel configuration | Channel config | Yes | Number, webhook, credentials |
| 2 | Inbound webhook receiver | Component | Yes | Signature-verified |
| 3 | Text message handling | Behaviour | Yes | Free text to the parser |
| 4 | Media handling | Behaviour | Yes | Images and documents stored; **voice notes are not handled in Phase 2** |
| 5 | Number resolution | Shared mechanism | Yes | Against sender identities |
| 6 | Conversation window rules | Behaviour | Yes | Template versus session message, respected on reply |

### Flow

1. The webhook receives a message and verifies its signature.
2. The number resolves to a client; unmatched goes to the unmatched queue.
3. Text is classified and parsed; media is stored and linked to the record.
4. A chase reply is sent inside the conversation window, or as an approved template outside it.

### Scenarios to test

- An unsigned or replayed webhook call is rejected.
- A message from a registered client number produces a demand record.
- A message from an unknown number lands in unmatched.
- A chase message outside the session window uses an approved template and is delivered.
- A voice note is recorded as received and routed to manual entry, not silently dropped.

## 4. Portal intake as a registered channel

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/admin/channels` · the portal form in [client portal](/docs/release-plan/part-2/client-portal)

### Scenarios to cover

The client portal becomes an intake channel like any other. A vacancy raised in the portal produces
**the same requisition record as one arriving by email**, marked as sourced from the portal — no
second code path, no second schema.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Portal channel | Channel config | Yes | Enabled per client |
| 2 | Structured submission | Payload | Yes | Already field-complete; no parsing needed |
| 3 | Source marking | Field | Yes | Channel = portal, with the submitting user |
| 4 | Completeness scoring | Shared | Yes | Runs the same as any channel |

### Flow

1. A portal submission arrives as an inbound record with a structured payload.
2. Sender resolution is trivial — the signed-in user's client.
3. Completeness scoring runs; a complete submission opens directly.

### Scenarios to test

- A portal requisition and an email requisition for the same vacancy differ only in source channel
  and raw payload.
- Disabling the portal channel for a client removes the option from their portal.
- The submitting client user is recorded on the requisition.

## 5. Parser dispatch and field extraction

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/inbound` · `/inbound/manual-entry`

### Scenarios to cover

A record classified as demand is handed to the parser for its channel, which extracts fields into
**the requisition schema** — the same schema the console form writes. What could not be extracted is
left blank rather than guessed, and the extraction is kept beside the raw payload so a disputed
requirement traces back to what the client actually sent.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parser per channel | Component | Yes | Dispatched by channel and intent |
| 2 | Parse result | Record | Yes | Extracted fields, per-field confidence, unparsed remainder |
| 3 | Requisition schema mapping | Mapping | Yes | Client, site, category, role, positions, experience, dates |
| 4 | Evidence link | Reference | Yes | Parse result to inbound record to raw payload |
| 5 | Review before open | Behaviour | Yes | A parsed requisition is reviewable before it goes live |
| 6 | Manual entry | Screen | Yes | A person keys in what the parser could not |

### Flow

1. The parser runs and produces a parse result with confidences.
2. Mapped fields create a draft requisition carrying its source evidence.
3. Complete drafts are scored in item 6; incomplete ones are chased or queued.
4. Manual entry lets a person complete a record from the raw payload beside the form.

### Scenarios to test

- A parsed requisition carries a resolvable link back to the raw payload.
- A field the parser could not read is blank, never invented.
- Manual entry shows the raw payload beside the form.
- The requisition produced is indistinguishable in shape from a console-created one.

## 6. Completeness scoring and the chase loop

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/inbound` · `/admin/channels`

### Scenarios to cover

*Complete* means different things for different candidate categories. The required-field set per
category defines it; a requisition below it is chased **on the channel it arrived on**, with a
configured message, a window, and an escalation target when the window passes.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Required-field set | Configuration | Yes | Per candidate category |
| 2 | Completeness score | Derived | Yes | Against that set |
| 3 | Follow-up configuration | Configuration | Yes | Message per channel, window, escalation target |
| 4 | Follow-up thread | Record | Yes | What was asked, when, and what came back |
| 5 | Reply matching | Behaviour | Yes | An inbound reply attaches to its thread and re-scores |
| 6 | Escalation | Action | Yes | To the configured internal owner on window expiry |
| 7 | State | Enum | Yes | `incomplete` → `awaiting_client` → `complete` |

### Flow

1. A parsed requisition is scored against its category's required-field set.
2. Complete → the requisition opens. Incomplete → a chase is sent on the arrival channel.
3. A reply attaches to the thread, re-parses and re-scores.
4. No reply inside the window escalates to the configured owner.

### Scenarios to test

- Changing the required-field set changes what is chased, without a deploy.
- A chase goes out on the channel the demand arrived on, not a default one.
- A reply re-scores the requisition and closes the thread when it completes it.
- Window expiry escalates once, to the configured target.
- A requisition never opens while it is below its required set.

## 7. The four operational queues

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/inbound` · `/inbound/unmatched` · `/inbound/manual-entry` · `/duplicates`

### Scenarios to cover

Everything the machine could not finish has a person's queue: what arrived, what could not be
matched to a client, what needs keying in, and what looks like the same vacancy twice.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inbound list | Screen | — | Everything received, with channel, client, intent, state |
| 2 | Unmatched senders | Screen | — | Resolve to a client, or add a sender identity from here |
| 3 | Manual entry | Screen | — | Raw payload beside the requisition form |
| 4 | Suspected duplicates | Screen | — | Same client, site, role and window |
| 5 | Duplicate resolution | Actions | — | Merge into the existing requisition, or keep both with a reason |
| 6 | Channel configuration | Screen | — | `/admin/channels`: enable, credentials, follow-up settings |

### Flow

1. Records land in the inbound list with their state.
2. An unmatched sender is resolved by a person, who can register the sender identity there and then;
   later messages from it resolve automatically.
3. Manual entry completes what could not be parsed.
4. A suspected duplicate is merged or kept, with the decision recorded.

### Scenarios to test

- Resolving an unmatched sender registers the identity and re-processes the record.
- A duplicate merged into an existing requisition keeps both source payloads attached.
- Keeping both requires a reason and records it.
- Every queue is filterable and exportable through the shared list engine.
- No record can sit in a queue with no owner and no state.
