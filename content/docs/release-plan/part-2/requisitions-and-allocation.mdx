## Purpose

> Introduction to the feature. **This is not the scope** — the scope is items 1 to 3 below.

The contractual line of business does not ask for a person, it asks for **a shift** — a date, a time
band, a site or a visit address, and a role. That is a different demand shape from a permanent
vacancy and it is what everything downstream in Phase 2 ranks, accepts, checks into and bills
against. The second thing here is smaller and often misread: the morning call already decides which
recruiter takes which vacancy. **This feature writes that decision down. It does not make it.**

## Release in this phase

1. Shift templates.
2. Shift demand lines.
3. Owner and allocation capture — the `D14` thin slice.

## 1. Shift templates

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/requisitions/:id`

### Scenarios to cover

A client needs the same pattern of shifts week after week. A template describes the pattern once —
role, time band, quantity, recurrence — and generates the demand lines from it, rather than someone
keying in forty rows.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Template | Record | Yes | Client, site, role, candidate category |
| 2 | Time band | Fields | Yes | Start time, end time, break rule |
| 3 | Quantity per occurrence | Number | Yes | How many people per shift |
| 4 | Recurrence | Rule | Yes | Days of week, date range |
| 5 | Care setting | Reference | Yes | Hospital, or home healthcare |
| 6 | Generation horizon | Setting | Yes | How far ahead lines are generated |
| 7 | Regeneration | Action | Yes | Re-generates unfilled future lines only |

### Flow

1. A user creates the template against a contractual requisition.
2. Generation produces one demand line per shift occurrence within the horizon.
3. Editing the template regenerates future unfilled lines and leaves accepted ones alone.
4. A rolling job extends generation as the horizon moves.

### Scenarios to test

- A template generates exactly the expected number of lines for its recurrence and range.
- Editing a template does not disturb an accepted or worked shift.
- The horizon job extends lines without duplicating existing ones.
- A home healthcare template requires a visit address on each generated line.

## 2. Shift demand lines

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/requisitions/:id` · `/board`

### Scenarios to cover

The demand line is the atom of the contractual chain: one shift, one date, one time band, one
quantity — ranked against, accepted against, checked into, and priced on a timesheet. Where the
workplace changes between shifts, **the visit address lives on the line**, not on the requisition.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demand line | Record | Yes | Requisition, date, start, end, quantity, role |
| 2 | Site or visit address | Reference or address | Yes | Site for facility work; **visit address for home care** |
| 3 | Fill state | Derived | Yes | Open, partly accepted, filled, cancelled |
| 4 | Acceptance link | Reference | — | The candidate acceptances against this line |
| 5 | Cancellation | Action | Yes | With a reason; notifies accepted candidates |
| 6 | Board rendering | Component | — | Shift demand visible alongside permanent demand |

### Flow

1. Lines are generated from a template or added individually.
2. Matching ranks candidates against a line; the app offers it to them.
3. Acceptances fill the line's quantity; the line closes when full.
4. Cancelling a line notifies anyone who accepted it and records the reason.

### Scenarios to test

- A line cannot be over-accepted beyond its quantity, under concurrency.
- A home care line without a visit address cannot be published.
- Cancelling a line notifies every accepted candidate exactly once.
- The board shows shift demand and permanent demand without conflating them.

## 3. Owner and allocation capture — `D14` thin

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/requisitions/:id` · `/board`

### Scenarios to cover

On the daily morning call, vacancies are handed to recruiters. This item records who was put on what
and when, **append-only**, so the allocation is answerable later and so Phase 3's reporting has the
owner field it needs.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Requisition owner | Reference | Yes | Already present from Phase 1; carried here |
| 2 | Allocation record | Append-only | Yes | Recruiter, allocated by, allocated at, note |
| 3 | Multiple recruiters | Behaviour | Yes | More than one recruiter may be allocated |
| 4 | Allocation view | Screen section | — | Current and historical allocations |
| 5 | Board filter | Filter | — | By allocated recruiter |

### Flow

1. After the morning call, a user records the allocations on the requisitions discussed.
2. Each allocation appends; nothing is overwritten.
3. The board can be filtered by recruiter, which is how the call is run the next day.

### Scenarios to test

- An allocation never overwrites its predecessor.
- Two recruiters can be allocated to one requisition and both are visible.
- Allocation history is readable in order with actors and times.
- Every allocation appears as an event, for Phase 3 reporting to read later.

<Callout type="warn" title="Thin means thin">
**No prioritisation engine, no suggested recruiter, no approval workflow.** Those are `D14` full —
[Phase 3 · Demand prioritisation and approval](/docs/release-plan/part-3/demand-prioritisation-and-approval).
Building them here spends Phase 2's schedule on Phase 3's capability.
</Callout>
