## Purpose

> Introduction to the feature. **This is not the scope** — the scope is items 1 and 2 below.

Phase 1 proved the path with one offer template and a straight interview flow. Phase 2 widens both to
the shape the business actually has: offers that differ by **state, role and skill** because
employment terms differ by state, and an interview process that survives the ordinary reality of
people moving and cancelling.

## Release in this phase

1. Offer templates by state, role and skill.
2. Reschedule and cancellation handling.

## 1. Offer templates by state, role and skill

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/offers` · `/admin/templates`

### Scenarios to cover

The right template is selected automatically from the placement's state, the role and the skill, so a
recruiter does not choose from a list of near-identical documents and pick the wrong one.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Template | Record | Yes | State, role, skill, category, version |
| 2 | Selection rule | Behaviour | Yes | Most specific match wins; no silent fallback |
| 3 | Clause variation | Content | Yes | State-specific statutory clauses |
| 4 | Variable resolution | Behaviour | Yes | From candidate, requisition, client, contract |
| 5 | Preview | Action | Yes | Before issue, as in Phase 1 |
| 6 | No-match handling | Behaviour | Yes | Refuses to issue rather than using a default |

### Flow

1. Templates are configured against state, role and skill combinations.
2. Offer creation resolves the template from the placement's attributes.
3. Where nothing matches, issue is blocked with a clear message.

### Scenarios to test

- The most specific template wins over a general one.
- No match blocks issue rather than silently using a generic document.
- A state-specific clause appears only in that state's offers.
- An issued offer retains its template version.

## 2. Reschedule and cancellation handling

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/interviews`

### Scenarios to cover

Interviews move. A reschedule keeps the interview and its history rather than creating a new one; a
cancellation records a reason and returns the candidate to where they were, and both sides are told.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reschedule | Action | — | New slots proposed, history retained |
| 2 | Reschedule reason | Reference | Yes | Who caused it: candidate, client, Apollo |
| 3 | Cancellation | Action | — | With a reason, at either party's request |
| 4 | Notifications | Triggers | Yes | Both parties told, through Phase 2 notifications |
| 5 | Reschedule count | Derived | — | Visible, for the pipeline to be worked |

### Flow

1. A reschedule proposes new slots on the same interview record.
2. Both sides are notified; the confirmed slot updates.
3. A cancellation records its reason and returns the candidate to the shortlist.

### Scenarios to test

- A reschedule keeps one interview record with its full history.
- Both parties are notified once per change, not once per slot.
- A cancelled interview returns the candidate to the shortlist without losing feedback already given.
- Reschedule reasons are reportable later, because they are structured.
