## Purpose

> Introduction to the feature. **This is not the scope** — the scope is items 1 to 4 below.

The money out. A **permanent placement** invoices once the client confirms at day 90 that the person
is still there — **not at placement**. Phase 1 builds that one path and the evidence behind it: who
confirmed, when, against which contract terms, and whether the client acknowledged or the window
expired. The chain here ends at *invoice raised, receivable open*.

## Release in this phase

1. Permanent invoice generation from the day-90 retention record.
2. The billing evidence view.
3. The receivable, with ageing displayed.
4. Duplicate prevention.

<Callout type="warn" title="No fallback, by design">
No contract terms in force at the trigger moment means permanent billing **does not run at all**.
That is not a defect to work around; it is the designed behaviour, and it is why contracts are
recorded before the first placement —
[Phase 1 · Clients and contracts](/docs/release-plan/part-1/clients-and-contracts).
</Callout>

## 1. Permanent invoice generation from the day-90 record

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/invoices` · `/invoices/:id`

### Scenarios to cover

The billing trigger arrives from placement carrying an immutable retention record. Billing resolves
the terms recorded on it, calculates the placement fee, and raises the invoice against the client —
at the terms in force **at the trigger moment**, not at today's terms.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Invoice | Record | Yes | Client, type (permanent), currency, dates, status |
| 2 | Invoice line | Record | Yes | Placement, candidate, role, fee basis, amount |
| 3 | Fee calculation | Derived | Yes | From the placement fee percentage snapshotted on the retention record |
| 4 | Contract version reference | Reference | Yes | Recorded on the invoice, not resolved at render time |
| 5 | Evidence reference | Reference | Yes | The retention record and the link that produced it |
| 6 | Invoice number | Sequence | Yes | Gapless per the agreed numbering rule |
| 7 | Invoice document | File | Yes | Generated and stored in the shared file store |
| 8 | Status | Enum | Yes | Draft, raised, sent, cancelled |
| 9 | Invoice list | Screen | — | Shared list engine, with export |

### Flow

1. The billing trigger arrives with the retention record reference.
2. Billing reads the snapshotted terms and computes the fee; money is decimal with a currency.
3. The invoice is created with its line, its evidence reference and its contract version id.
4. The invoice document is generated and stored; the invoice moves to raised.
5. A receivable is opened in item 3, and the invoice event is recorded.

### Scenarios to test

- The fee matches the snapshotted percentage, not the current rate card.
- An invoice always carries a resolvable evidence reference.
- A trigger with no terms produces no invoice and a visible exception for finance.
- Invoice numbering has no gaps and no duplicates under concurrent triggers.
- Cancelling an invoice records who cancelled it and why, and leaves the record readable.

## 2. The billing evidence view

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/billing/evidence`

### Scenarios to cover

A client queries a charge months later. The evidence view answers it without anyone opening a
database: **who confirmed, when, against which contract terms, and whether the client acknowledged or
the window expired** — each item linked to the record that proves it.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidence panel | Screen | — | One per invoice, reachable from the invoice |
| 2 | Confirmation detail | Read-only | Yes | Responder, timestamp, or the expiry that deemed it |
| 3 | Terms applied | Read-only | Yes | Contract version, fee basis, percentage |
| 4 | Link history | Read-only | Yes | From the link log: issued, sent, opened, answered or expired |
| 5 | Placement detail | Read-only | Yes | Candidate, site, role, joining date, day-90 date |
| 6 | Export | Action | — | The evidence pack for one invoice |

### Flow

1. Finance opens an invoice and follows through to its evidence.
2. Every element resolves from immutable records, not from current operational data.
3. An export produces a pack that can be sent to the client.

### Scenarios to test

- Every invoice raised in Phase 1 has complete evidence; none is partial.
- The evidence renders identically a year later, after rate cards and contracts have changed.
- A deemed outcome is labelled as deemed, never as an acknowledgement.
- The export contains everything on screen and nothing about another invoice.

## 3. The receivable, with ageing displayed

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/receivables`

### Scenarios to cover

A raised invoice opens a receivable, and the receivable ages against the payment terms recorded on
the contract version. Phase 1 **displays** ageing; it does not chase, reconcile or record payments.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receivable | Record | Yes | Invoice, client, amount, due date, status |
| 2 | Due date | Derived | Yes | From the contract payment terms, not a flat rule |
| 3 | Ageing bands | Display | Yes | Current, and the agreed overdue bands |
| 4 | Receivable list | Screen | — | Shared list engine, with export |
| 5 | Status | Enum | Yes | Open, cancelled |

### Flow

1. Raising an invoice opens a receivable with a due date from the contract terms.
2. The list shows outstanding amounts by client and by age.
3. Cancelling an invoice cancels its receivable.

### Scenarios to test

- The due date follows the contract payment terms for that client.
- Ageing bands are computed against the due date, in `Asia/Kolkata` business dates.
- The receivable total reconciles with the raised invoices.
- Cancelling an invoice removes its amount from the outstanding total, with the record retained.

## 4. Duplicate prevention

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/invoices`

### Scenarios to cover

One retention record produces **one** invoice, whatever happens to the trigger: a retry, a redelivered
event, a second click, or an operator re-queueing a dead-lettered job.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trigger idempotency key | Field | Yes | Derived from the retention record |
| 2 | Uniqueness constraint | Database | Yes | One invoice per trigger, enforced by the database |
| 3 | Repeat handling | Behaviour | Yes | A repeat returns the existing invoice, and records the attempt |
| 4 | Exception queue | Screen state | — | Triggers that could not invoice, with the reason |

### Flow

1. Every trigger carries an idempotency key.
2. Invoice creation is guarded by a uniqueness constraint on that key.
3. A repeat is a no-op that returns the existing invoice and records the attempt.
4. A trigger that cannot invoice — no terms in force — lands in the exception queue with its reason.

### Scenarios to test

- The same trigger delivered five times produces one invoice.
- Concurrent delivery of the same trigger produces one invoice, proven under load.
- A dead-lettered job re-queued after a successful invoice does not produce a second.
- An exception carries a reason a finance user can act on.
