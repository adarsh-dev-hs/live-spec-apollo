## Purpose

> Introduction to the feature. **This is not the scope** — the scope is items 1 and 2 below.

Earlier phases made the trial work and made it visible. Phase 3 makes it **operable at volume**: one
dashboard across every category and care setting, and a negative answer that reaches a person by
itself rather than waiting to be noticed. A placement lost at day 40 is a permanent invoice that never
happens.

## Release in this phase

1. The trial dashboard at width.
2. Escalation on negative responses.

## 1. The trial dashboard at width

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/trial`

### Scenarios to cover

Every live trial across the three candidate categories and both care settings, in one view: which
check-ins are done, due and missed, which answers were negative, and which placements are approaching
their day-90 trigger.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trial view | Screen | — | All live placements, filterable by category, care setting, client, owner |
| 2 | Check-in status matrix | Component | — | Day 0 / 7 / 30 / 60 / 90 per placement |
| 3 | Negative-answer indicator | Derived | Yes | Visible without opening the placement |
| 4 | Day-90 approach | Derived | Yes | Placements nearing the trigger, for finance |
| 5 | Missed check-ins | Derived | Yes | Aged, and assignable |
| 6 | Export | Action | — | Through the shared list engine |

### Flow

1. The dashboard reads every live placement and its trial events.
2. Status is shown per event, with negatives and misses surfaced.
3. Filters let one owner work their own trials.

### Scenarios to test

- Totals reconcile with the placement records, per category and care setting.
- A missed check-in appears as missed and is assignable to a person.
- Placements approaching day 90 are visible in advance to finance.
- Export matches the on-screen filter.

## 2. Escalation on negative responses

**ClickUp item** — *link to be added*

**Screens** — `/trial` · `/placements/:id`

### Scenarios to cover

A negative answer — not being paid on time, no preceptor, a problem at the site — **raises an
escalation to a named owner automatically**, with a response window, rather than waiting to be read
on a dashboard.

### Elements

| # | Element | Type | Mandatory | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Escalation rule | Configuration | Yes | Which answers escalate, to whom, in what window |
| 2 | Escalation record | Record | Yes | Trial response, owner, raised at, state |
| 3 | Notification | Trigger | Yes | Through the notification layer |
| 4 | Resolution | Action | Yes | Outcome and a mandatory note |
| 5 | Second-level escalation | Rule | Yes | On window expiry, to the configured next owner |
| 6 | Reporting hook | Event | Yes | Escalations are events, so they reach the marts |

### Flow

1. A negative answer matches an escalation rule and raises an escalation.
2. The owner is notified and works it to a resolution.
3. An unresolved escalation escalates again at window expiry.

### Scenarios to test

- Every configured negative answer raises exactly one escalation.
- An unresolved escalation escalates once at expiry, not repeatedly.
- Resolution requires an outcome note.
- Escalations appear in the reporting marts alongside trial survival.
